Fariborz Aref · Labs in sociology

Sociology you can run.

Two labs that turn central ideas of sociology into models you can operate. The Inequality Lab works with published data on income, wealth, and welfare states. The Methods Lab uses simulation to show how sound calculations lead to unsound conclusions.

Written for students, colleagues, and anyone who would rather see an argument work than take it on trust.

Standards

What every demonstration owes its reader.

The same three commitments hold on every page, so a student can check the work rather than trust it.

01

Published data

Figures come from the World Inequality Report, the OECD, the World Bank, the UN, UNDP, and the Maddison historical series. Each source is listed with the demonstration that uses it.

02

Stated assumptions

Where a chart interpolates, approximates, or simulates, a note beneath it says so and explains how.

03

Built for teaching

Each demonstration fits a single class session, comes with notes for instructors, and connects to the courses that use it.

Courses

The labs in the classroom.

Each course follows the same sequence, from question to design to evidence to a claim that holds up under questioning. The labs are where several of those steps are practiced.
