The Methods Lab

How a claim is earned.

Seven demonstrations of places where a correct calculation leads to a wrong conclusion. In each, the error lies in the design or the reading of the analysis, and the arithmetic is fine.

All use simulated data, so the true answer is known in advance. The last three take their settings from published comparative research on OECD countries.

I.Demonstration 1

Simpson's Paradox

Durkheim held that a society is a reality of its own, distinct from the sum of its members. Simpson's paradox is a statistical version of that claim: an aggregate relationship can run opposite to the relationship inside every one of its parts.

Does social spending reduce inequality? Pool the societies below and the answer appears to be no. Then separate them by structural pressure, meaning the inequality they face before any policy acts. Societies under more pressure spend more and stay more unequal.

SimulatedThirty societies in three groups. The structure follows a known problem in comparative research: welfare effort responds to pre-existing inequality, so spending is endogenous to the outcome it is meant to affect.

The pooled slope is computed correctly and answers the wrong question. Deciding which comparison is meaningful is a theoretical decision made before any model is run.

CiteAref, F. (2026). Simpson’s Paradox. The Methods Lab. https://fariborzaref.github.io/methods#simpson

For instructors

Goal. Show that a pooled relationship can reverse within every group.

Ask the class to answer aloud. Which slope answers whether spending reduces inequality among comparable societies?

  1. The pooled slope
  2. The slope within each group

Answer. The slope within each group, which compares societies facing the same structural pressure.

II.Demonstration 2

The Ecological Fallacy

W. S. Robinson (1950) used the 1930 U.S. census. Across the 48 states, the share of foreign-born residents correlated with higher literacy. Among individuals, foreign birth correlated with lower literacy. Immigrants had settled in states where literacy was already high.

The simulation below reproduces Robinson's structure. Switch from states to people: the same population, counted at a different level, gives the opposite answer.

SimulatedEight states of thirty people each. Higher-literacy states attract more immigrants, and immigrants are less literate than the native-born within every state.

The unit of analysis limits the conclusion. Data on places support claims about places, and claims about persons need data on persons.

CiteAref, F. (2026). The Ecological Fallacy. The Methods Lab. https://fariborzaref.github.io/methods#ecological

For instructors

Goal. Match the unit of analysis to the claim.

Ask the class to answer aloud. State data show that states with more immigrants have higher literacy. Can we conclude that immigrants are more literate?

  1. Yes
  2. No

Answer. No. The data describe states. Claims about persons need data on persons.

III.Demonstration 3

What 95% Confidence Means

Jerzy Neyman introduced the confidence interval in 1937 as a statement about a procedure: repeated many times, the method captures the true value at a known rate. It is among the most misread results in applied statistics.

Forty percent of this population holds a view. Draw surveys and compute an interval from each. Watch how often the intervals capture 40%, and notice that a missed interval looks no different from a successful one.

SimulatedSimple random samples from a population with a true proportion of 0.40. Intervals use the normal approximation, p̂ ± z·√(p̂(1−p̂)/n). The latest 60 intervals are shown.

The 95% describes the long run of the method. Any single interval either contains the true value or does not, and the data in hand cannot say which.

CiteAref, F. (2026). What 95% Confidence Means. The Methods Lab. https://fariborzaref.github.io/methods#confidence

For instructors

Goal. Interpret a confidence interval correctly.

Ask the class to answer aloud. Which statement about a 95% confidence interval is correct?

  1. There is a 95% chance the true value lies inside this interval
  2. 95% of intervals made this way contain the true value

Answer. The second. The 95% describes the procedure across repeated samples.

IV.Demonstration 4

The Garden of Forking Paths

Andrew Gelman and Eric Loken borrowed the phrase from Borges in 2013. A researcher who chooses among many reasonable analyses after seeing the data can find significance in pure noise, without any intent to deceive.

Below is one simulated survey of 400 people. Nothing in it predicts the outcome. Each analysis tests one predictor in one subgroup, the kind of choice every dataset invites. Run them and see how soon a publishable result appears.

SimulatedOutcome and ten predictors are independent standard normal variables. Subgroups are random. Each p-value comes from a test of the correlation coefficient. Analyses that share data are correlated, so the independence figure is an approximation.

A p-value means what it claims only when the analysis was fixed in advance. The remedy is a pre-registered plan, or a full report of every path that was tried.

CiteAref, F. (2026). The Garden of Forking Paths. The Methods Lab. https://fariborzaref.github.io/methods#forking

For instructors

Goal. Recognize how multiple testing produces false findings.

Ask the class to answer aloud. A study reports one significant result out of twenty tests. This is

  1. strong evidence of an effect
  2. about what chance alone would produce

Answer. About what chance alone would produce. One in twenty is the expected false-positive rate at the 5% level.

V.Demonstration 5

Between and Within

Multilevel models divide variation into two parts: differences between countries and change within countries over time. The share that lies between countries is the intraclass correlation. It tells a researcher how much of an outcome belongs to national institutions that rarely change.

The panel below follows 27 OECD countries from 2002 to 2021. It is generated from the fixed effects and variance components of a published mixed-effects model. Choose an outcome, then remove each country’s average to see how little of the variation is change over time.

Shares are computed from the published country and residual standard deviations; random slopes are omitted. The country lines are simulated from those estimates and do not show real countries.

Across OECD countries, most of the variation in inequality, employment, and health is a lasting difference between nations. A crisis moves the smaller, within-country part, which is why comparative models must separate the two.

CiteAref, F. (2026). Between and Within. The Methods Lab. https://fariborzaref.github.io/methods#between

For instructors

Goal. Separate differences between countries from change within them.

Ask the class to answer aloud. Most of the variation in OECD Gini values from 2002 to 2021 lies

  1. between countries
  2. within countries over time

Answer. Between countries: about four-fifths in the published model.

VI.Demonstration 6

Twenty Years Are Not Twenty Observations

National statistics change slowly: this year’s Gini is close to last year’s. A model of OECD welfare regimes estimated the year-to-year correlation of the Gini index at 0.957, and so used generalized estimating equations with an AR(1) working correlation.

The series below has no trend at all. Raise the year-to-year correlation and watch two things: how little information twenty persistent observations carry, and how often an ordinary trend test reports a trend that is not there.

Equivalent independent observations
False trends, naive test

MethodTwenty annual values from a stationary AR(1) process with no trend. Equivalent observations are n² divided by the sum of all pairwise correlations. False-trend rates come from 600 simulated series tested with ordinary least squares at the 5% level. The 0.957 preset is the AR(1) correlation reported for the Gini model in Aref (2024), International Review of Economics 71.

Repeated measures of a slow-moving outcome contain far less information than their number suggests. Standard errors that ignore this are too small, and trends appear where there are none.

CiteAref, F. (2026). Twenty Years Are Not Twenty Observations. The Methods Lab. https://fariborzaref.github.io/methods#persistence

For instructors

Goal. Understand why autocorrelation shrinks the information in panel data.

Ask the class to answer aloud. Twenty years of a slow-moving national statistic contain

  1. twenty independent observations
  2. far fewer independent observations

Answer. Far fewer. At a correlation of 0.957, twenty years carry the information of about one independent observation.

VII.Demonstration 7

Twenty-Seven Countries, Many Variables

Cross-national research works with few cases. Comparative studies of the OECD often have 20 to 30 countries with complete data, and every control variable spends part of that small sample.

The outcome below is random noise for 27 countries. Add control variables, each also pure noise, and watch R² rise toward 1 while adjusted R² stays near zero on average. Draw new data to see how unstable a single small sample is.

SimulatedOutcome and predictors are independent standard normal variables for 27 cases, the number of OECD countries in the two published studies. Predictors are added one at a time to the same data.

In small-N comparison, a good fit is easy to manufacture. The number of predictors must stay far below the number of countries, and fit statistics should be read against what noise alone would produce.

CiteAref, F. (2026). Twenty-Seven Countries, Many Variables. The Methods Lab. https://fariborzaref.github.io/methods#smalln

For instructors

Goal. Recognize overfitting when cases are few.

Ask the class to answer aloud. Adding a variable of pure noise to a regression makes R²

  1. rise
  2. fall
  3. stay the same

Answer. Rise, or at least never fall. That is why adjusted R² and restraint matter with few countries.

Each failure here produces a clean number and a confident sentence. Judgment about structure, level, and the number of tests performed decides whether that sentence is true.
